An AI agent that spends real money under a rule you wrote — corrects its own numbers with cited evidence, and stops dead when the fix would need a new rule.

01 · Where the problem was found

I spent a summer
carrying fridges.

A job booked as a straight drop. More often: doors off, the fridge on its side, an hour navigating a stranger's home. One afternoon an old man led us into his garden to find his water supply.

The system that paid me saw five fields. Crew ID, date, total time, start, postcode. An hour manoeuvring a fridge and an hour sitting in traffic were the same number.

The work was real. Nothing in the instrument could say it.

02 · The distinction everything rests on

Two ways an objective fails.

Learned objectives detect only the first. They converge, with excellent metrics, on an incomplete theory of the job.

03 · Who writes the definition of good

Three ways to hold an objective.

Someone else writes it

Provenance,
no standing

There's a document and an author — but the people who feel the gaps can't add the terms. Arguable in principle, unarguable in practice.

Nobody writes it

Learned
from feedback

No document, no vocabulary to amend — only weights. Misalignment surfaces after the harm, as a behaviour someone disliked. Scales beautifully. Steers nowhere.

The cost-bearer writes it

Provenance
and standing

The author can be asked why, and the author is the one who pays. Both failure modes get a repair channel.

Observe. Record. Cluster.

The agent holds commit authority over numbers within written scope, and none at all over words. A civil servant, not a legislator.

06 · The analogy

A junior with a company card.

What they may do

Spend inside the limit you set. Notice the supplier raised prices, show you six invoices, and adjust the budget line — because the receipts say so, and the new figure is still inside the range you wrote.

What they may never do

Invent a new budget line. When a fee appears that no existing line covers, they don't quietly absorb it and they don't approve it. They bring you the receipts and ask. That question is the product.

Most AI agents are the junior who either freezes, or spends and tells you after.

07 · What you're about to watch

A freelance rate card
that corrects itself.

  • You write the card. A rate, its bounds, and a ⟂ if the price is a guess — signed on-chain.
  • The agent works. Invoices within your rate get paid, each citing the rule that authorised it.
  • The world disagrees. Invoices above your rate are refused and filed as residuals — your unpaid hours, on the record.
  • The number corrects. Six residuals agree; the agent moves the rate to the measured median, inside your bounds. The ⟂ closes.
  • The word is missing. The same contradiction returns. No rate absorbs it. The agent proposes platform-fee — and stops.
  • You sign. The rate card grows a line item, with your address on the event.
08 · Why Monad

The substrate,
not a feature.

  • Public state, not a config file. Anyone reads the objective without asking us.
  • Authority enforced by the EVM. The agent can't author a term because the transaction reverts — a consensus rule, not a promise.
  • Real value with a citation. Payments revert if they can't name their rule or exceed the ceiling.
  • The UI is a chain reader. No database. Legibility comes from the substrate.
  • Speed makes it watchable. Six transactions and a correction inside ninety seconds. At twelve-second blocks this demo is a progress bar.

Honest version: nothing here is Monad-exclusive. What it needs is an EVM chain fast and cheap enough to run the whole loop in front of an audience — which is the difference between a paper and a product.
