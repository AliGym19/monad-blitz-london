Monad is the substrate, not a feature.

The claim this project makes is that a written objective fails at an address that is inspectable. Monad is the substrate that makes agentic work legible, checkable and sovereign. Everything below is what the chain does that a database could not.

01

What the chain provides

five things, each tied to the loop
1

The rate card is on-chain state, not a config file.

Terms, parameters, residuals and proposals are storage mappings in AO.sol. Anyone can read the objective function without asking us for it, and no one — including the author — can quietly rewrite what it used to say.

ties toThe prior the agent reads at the start of every round is fetched from this storage, not from the agent's own memory. It holds no private beliefs.
2

Authority separation is enforced by the EVM, not by trust.

onlyAuthor and onlySRI are require statements comparing msg.sender. The agent cannot author a term because the transaction reverts — a consensus rule, not a policy it might choose to ignore. Monad's EVM equivalence means this is ordinary Solidity: no novel trust assumptions to audit.

ties toThe moment the agent diagnoses a missing line item, it opens a proposal and stops. Not because it was well-behaved — because authoring would revert.
3

Payments are real value, with a citation attached.

citedPay moves native MON out of the contract's treasury. The agent isn't logging that it paid — it paid. And the transfer reverts if it cannot name the term authorising it or stay under the authored ceiling. The economic stake is what makes the constraint bite.

ties toEvery "paid, within your rate" line in the run log is a real transfer; every "over your rate — logged, not paid" is the agent declining to breach and filing evidence instead.
4

Every correction is an event, so the interface is just a chain reader.

These pages hold no database. They call termCount(), params(i) and getLogs() against Monad RPC and render whatever the chain says. The ledger of payments, residuals, commits and ratifications is the block history — legibility comes free from the substrate rather than from us promising to log honestly.

ties toThe agent page recomputes the diagnosis arithmetic in your browser from on-chain residuals. Same inputs, same verdict — you can check its homework without trusting our rendering.
5

Speed is what makes the loop watchable.

A single round fires a rapid sequence: six invoices, then residuals, then a commit — and each confirms fast enough to narrate live. On a chain with twelve-second blocks, a six-transaction round is a minute of dead air and the demo collapses into a progress bar. Cheap gas is why running a full demo costs pocket change and nobody has to ration the evidence.

ties toThe repair loop only reads as a loop if the correction lands while the audience is still watching. Finality speed is the difference between a claim and a demonstration.

The honest version

What we don't claim

AO uses nothing Monad-exclusive. There are no parallel-execution tricks, no custom precompiles, no dependency on anything another EVM chain lacks. Claiming otherwise would be the kind of unearned assertion this whole project argues against.

What it needs is an EVM chain fast and cheap enough that a full loop — author, act, correct, escalate, sign — runs at conversational speed, in front of an audience, with real value moving. Monad is what makes the argument demonstrable rather than merely described.

That is not a small thing. The difference between "this system could correct itself in public" and watching it do so in ninety seconds is the entire difference between a paper and a product.
